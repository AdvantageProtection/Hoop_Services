01

Most directors pay for cover out of money already taxed

The conversation belongs in the tax planning meeting. It just rarely gets started.

Almost every limited company director you act for already holds personal life insurance or income protection. In the overwhelming majority of cases it's paid from their personal account, out of income that has already suffered tax and National Insurance.

The company could pay for the same protection instead — as a legitimate, allowable business expense, and without the director being taxed on it as a benefit in kind. For a director paying a few hundred pounds a month, that's a five-figure swing over the life of the policy.

They just don't know the option exists. And because it sits squarely inside the tax planning work you already do, it lands with you long before it lands with a broker.

02

What actually gets arranged

Both are company-funded. Both are designed to be treated as a business expense rather than a P11D benefit. Both are arranged properly, under advice, with the tax position documented.

Product A

Relevant Life Cover

A single-life policy written for a director or key employee. The company pays the premium and claims it as an allowable expense. The benefit is paid to nominated beneficiaries, normally outside the estate.

The director stops funding personal life cover out of post-tax income and the company takes it on instead.

Must meet HMRC relevant life conditions · under age 75

Product B

Executive Income Protection

Replaces a director's earnings if illness or injury stops them working. Premiums are paid by the company as an allowable expense, and the claim pays out to fund salary or sick pay.

It's the corporate version of the personal income protection most directors already hold — and already resent paying for.

Structured to meet HMRC conditions · limited term options

Cost to the director A free, whole-of-market review.

03

Why a director says yes in one conversation

  • Same cover, better funded The protection stays in place. It's simply paid for by the business rather than out of taxed salary.
  • Immediate, quantified saving The saving is calculable in the meeting — typically thousands a year for a director already paying for personal cover.
  • Allowable business expense Premiums are deductible for the company when the policy is set up correctly.
  • No benefit-in-kind charge Structured to sit outside the P11D regime, so there's no personal tax hit on the benefit.
  • Benefit outside the estate Relevant Life Cover pays to nominated beneficiaries via trustees, keeping the sum out of the estate in the normal course.
  • Advice at no cost A full, independent review and recommendation, funded by provider commission rather than the client.

06

The questions accountants actually ask

Does this create work for me?

No. You flag the relevance and make the introduction. We fact-find, research, recommend, apply and service. You're copied in, not drafted in.

What does it cost my client?

Nothing for the advice. The review is free and independent. The client pays the provider's premium, funded through the company rather than personally — usually for less than they were already paying out of taxed income.

How much do we get?

A worthwhile share of the commission on every case placed, agreed up front and paid as standard. We'll put the figure in writing before you refer anyone.

Who carries the regulatory responsibility?

We do. Advantage Financial Solutions is directly authorised by the FCA, 965036. We're the adviser of record, we hold the file, and we handle the compliance.

What if the director already has cover?

That's the usual starting point, and usually the best case. We review what's in place and show what a corporate-funded equivalent looks like. Sometimes existing cover can be moved; sometimes it's better kept and supplemented. They get told either way.

Are the tax advantages guaranteed?

Not guaranteed — they depend on the policy meeting HMRC conditions and on the director's circumstances. That's precisely why it's arranged under advice, with the tax position documented on file, rather than sold off a shelf.
